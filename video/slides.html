0:00 – 0:25 • Problem Context
The Intermittent Water Supply Crisis
Why standard commercial leak detection fails across the Global South
1/3rd Treated Water Lost: Indian cities lose 30-50% of municipal treated water to undetected underground pipe bursts before reaching households.
Intermittent Supply (2-4 hrs/day): Unlike Western grids with 24/7 continuous pressurized flow, water runs only a few hours each morning and evening.
Pipes Drain & Refill: Continuous hydraulic leak algorithms assume steady pressure profiles. Intermittent filling generates massive pressure swings and air transients that break standard algorithms.

0:25 – 0:55 • The Demand Masking Conundrum
Figure 1: Demand Swings Mask Leak Signals
Consumer demand swings by ±10%, creating identical pressure drops to pipe bursts
Natural Demand Volatility (±10%): Day-to-day consumer drawing fluctuates by ±10% depending on weather, morning routines, and storage tank refilling.
Pressure Confounding: In an intermittent network, a +8% demand surge drops junction head by ~1.0 metre. A medium leak (7.5% of supply) causes an identical ~1.1 metre drop!
Plain Threshold Failure: A naive fixed pressure threshold confuses demand swings with leaks, catching only 49% of leaks at 5% False Alarm Rate.
Hydraulic Confounding
ΔP_demand ≈ -7.2 × (g - 1.0) ≈ ±0.9 m head
ΔP_leak ≈ -1.1 m head

Honest Limits & Municipal Roadmap
Clear boundary conditions for practical deployment
What Boond Solves
• Eliminates demand swing false alarms under intermittent supply.
• Boosts detection from 49% → 78% for medium and large leaks.
• Narrows leak origin to top-5 neighbourhood candidate junctions.
• Works with modest municipal sensor budgets (10-20 per DMA).
Honest Limitations
• Simulated: Validated on WNTR EPANET models; requires city pilot data.
• Small Leaks: Very small leaks (<2.5%) remain hard to detect with 5 sensors.
• Neighbourhood-Level: Identifies the pipe cluster; requires field acoustic pinpointing.
